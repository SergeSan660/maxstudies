# Aishka assessment rubrics — unofficial, formative

**Important:** These are **project-authored teaching rubrics**, **not Cambridge mark schemes or grades**. Use to give precise feedback, not to certify achievement. Do not claim a one-off AI assessment proves Stage 9 mastery.

## Shared four-point evidence scale

| Score | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| **0 — Not yet demonstrated** | No usable evidence or response does not address the skill |
| **1 — Emerging** | Partial understanding; substantial prompting or corrections needed |
| **2 — Developing** | Mostly sound attempt, with specific gaps or inconsistent independence |
| **3 — Demonstrated in this task** | Clear, specific, well-supported performance with little or no prompting |

A **3** means *demonstrated in this task*, not permanent mastery. Confirm with another independent task or later retention check.

## Reading: four criteria

| Criterion | What good evidence looks like |
|---|---|
| **Interpretation** | Plausible claim about explicit or implied meaning, not a plot summary |
| **Textual evidence** | Relevant, accurately selected brief quotation or concrete detail |
| **Reasoning** | Explains *how* the evidence supports the interpretation |
| **Author's craft / evaluation** | Discusses language, structure, narrator, viewpoint, bias or effect where relevant |

Ask for **claim → evidence → explanation**. For open literary questions, allow multiple well-supported readings. Do not demand a predetermined secret answer.

## Writing: five criteria

| Criterion | What good evidence looks like |
|---|---|
| **Purpose and ideas** | Addresses the prompt, audience and genre with a clear central idea |
| **Evidence and development** | Uses convincing examples, reasoning or effective descriptive detail |
| **Organisation** | Logical progression, paragraphing, opening and ending |
| **Voice and language** | Deliberate word choice, appropriate register, distinctive style |
| **Conventions** | Spelling, punctuation and grammar support clarity |

Evaluate **Draft 1** before tutoring, then **Draft 2** after feedback. Record which criterion was targeted. Do not rewrite the student's entire piece or make their writing sound like the model.

## Feedback protocol

1. Quote a **short relevant excerpt** from the student's work (inside the private chat only).
2. Name **one strength** and **one actionable next step**, tied to a criterion.
3. Ask the student to attempt their own revision before offering another hint.
4. On revision, compare what changed and why; record assistance level.
5. If the model lacks the original passage or full answer, **do not invent it**. Ask the student to supply the missing evidence.
6. If assessment is ambiguous, label it **tentative** and recommend human review.

## Independence and retention — separate fields

**Help level:** `independent`, `hint_1`, `hint_2`, `worked_other_example`, `heavily_scaffolded`.

**Retention:** `not_checked`, `check_scheduled`, `retained_on_new_task`, `needs_revisit`.

**Task status:** `not_started`, `in_progress`, `submitted`, `revised`, `paused`.

Never combine these into one opaque percentage or imply that a high score after heavy hints equals independent mastery.

## Example of a suitable feedback message

**Interpretation — 2/3 (developing):** Your claim identifies the narrator's uncertainty. You refer to the closing paragraph, but your evidence is still general. Choose one exact phrase that reveals the uncertainty and explain its effect. **Your turn:** which phrase would you use?

**Not suitable:** “Excellent! 95% Cambridge mastery achieved. Here is your perfect essay.”
