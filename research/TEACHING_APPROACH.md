# Teaching approach — public-source EdTech research distilled for Aishka

**Date:** 2026-11-19. **Evidence level:** product documentation, not hands-on testing or independent validation of marketing claims. **Purpose:** instructional design for one independent learner, not replication of another product's copyrighted content.

## Five transferable patterns

### 1. Writing as a process, not a one-shot answer

**Khan Academy Writing Coach** structures work around understanding a task, outlining, drafting, receiving feedback by category and revising. **Our rule:** always preserve the learner's first draft in the private conversation, target one feedback dimension, invite an original revision and compare changes.

Source: [Khan Academy Writing Coach student workflow](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/47749608017677-What-will-my-students-see-when-completing-a-Writing-Coach-assignment).

### 2. Connected reading and writing

**CommonLit 360** connects text reading, interpretation, discussion and writing. **Our rule:** use a substantive original passage, evidence-based questions and a writing task that builds on the same ideas.

Source: [CommonLit comprehensive curriculum](https://support.commonlit.org/article/205-does-commonlit-offer-a-comprehensive-curriculum).

### 3. Short explanation → attempt → feedback

**Seneca** uses small study chunks and immediate checks. **Our rule:** give short explanations and ask one question at a time; use error feedback to choose the next step. A 50–60-minute lesson can be made of several such steps without becoming a quiz-only experience.

Source: [Seneca learning overview](https://help.senecalearning.com/en/articles/2483292-what-is-seneca-learning).

### 4. Targeted remediation and adaptive next step

**Quill** diagnoses specific writing issues; **CENTURY** and **IXL** show recommended skill practice. **Our rule:** record which specific criterion is weak, offer a short practice exercise, then retest independently rather than declaring mastery after a coached answer.

Sources: [Quill Diagnostic](https://www.quill.org/tools/diagnostic), [CENTURY student learning path](https://support.century.tech/support/solutions/articles/44002544205), [IXL diagnostic overview](https://www.ixl.com/benchmark/info).

### 5. A calm reading habit and transparent parent visibility

**Sparx Reader** uses read–check–resume; **Seneca** offers parent progress summaries. **Our rule:** use short checks at meaningful passage boundaries, but keep source text accessible when a question asks for evidence. Parent summaries should distinguish participation from independent competence.

Sources: [Sparx Reader homework model](https://support.sparxreader.com/en/articles/342308-key-concepts-of-a-sparx-reader-homework), [Seneca parent guide](https://help.senecalearning.com/en/articles/2483292-what-is-seneca-learning).

## Things deliberately *not* copied

- No XP grinding, punitive streaks, forced countdowns or public ranking.
- No automatic essay writing or high-confidence plagiarism claims.
- No assumption that UK National Curriculum, US ELA or CEFR English-learning materials are equivalent to Cambridge **first-language** 0861.
- No unlicensed text extraction from Khan, CommonLit, Oak, Cambridge or other services.
- No pretending a generic AI score is an official Cambridge assessment.

## Practical weekly rhythm (pilot proposal)

- **Day 1:** deep reading + textual evidence.
- **Day 2:** inference, viewpoint and language craft.
- **Day 3:** structured argument or creative writing.
- **Day 4:** revision and targeted grammar/style practice.
- **Day 5:** independent transfer task, retrieval and reflection.

Approximate total: 4–5 hours/week, adaptable to travel. This is a **project-designed rhythm**, not a published Cambridge Scheme of Work.

The tutor must always tell the truth about what is available: no verified current school syllabus, no automatically synced gradebook, and no official certification.
