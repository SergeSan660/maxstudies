# Four-week independent English pilot (20 sessions)

**Status:** original **pilot plan**, not Cambridge's official Scheme of Work, not a complete Stage 9 curriculum and not yet validated against the student's current school version. Aishka may use this plan to run a first month of private, independent English study while tracking actual evidence.

**Frequency:** 5 sessions/week, ~50–60 minutes/session; optional 15-minute travel version. Use the [assessment rubrics](../assessment/RUBRICS.md) and [session log](../assessment/SESSION_LOG.md). Ask one question at a time. Do not claim every session is a completed Cambridge objective.

## Week 1 — Understand, infer and revise

| Day | Focus | Learner output | Evidence to observe |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [Original diagnostic](../content/DIAGNOSTIC_01.md) | Five reading answers + 200–300-word first draft | Literal understanding, inference, evidence, voice; initial independence |
| 2 | [The Museum of Unsent Letters](../content/LESSON_01.md) | Text analysis + argument or creative response | Perspective, narrative structure, justified interpretation |
| 3 | How narrators shape trust | Tutor creates a **new original** 350–500-word first-person passage with an unreliable narrator; learner annotates 3 clues | Narrator vs author, inference, precise evidence |
| 4 | Revise a paragraph | Learner revises one Week 1 draft with rubric guidance; explain 3 deliberate changes | Revision independence, structure and word choice |
| 5 | Independent transfer | Tutor creates a new short passage; learner analyses it **without hints**, then reviews Week 1 errors | Retention, independent use of evidence |

## Week 2 — Argument, evidence and counterargument

| Day | Focus | Learner output | Evidence to observe |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | Claim vs opinion | Compare two short original arguments on a low-stakes school/community issue | Identifying claims, reasons, assumptions |
| 7 | Strong and weak evidence | Rank evidence examples; explain limitations | Evaluating support and source credibility |
| 8 | Build an argument | Three-point outline + independent 250-word argument | Purpose, logic, audience and evidence |
| 9 | Counterargument and revision | Write a fair counterargument, revise the earlier essay | Intellectual fairness, cohesion and precision |
| 10 | Retention challenge | New prompt, 15-minute independent mini-essay, then reflection | Transfer without tutor-written text |

## Week 3 — Language, bias and text structure

| Day | Focus | Learner output | Evidence to observe |
|---|---|---|---|
| 11 | Tone and connotation | Compare two **original** reports of the same event | Word choice, tone, audience effect |
| 12 | Perspective and bias | Explain what each narrator includes and omits | Bias vs evidence, competing interpretations |
| 13 | Structure and pacing | Reorder a short original scene and justify the effect | Opening, turning point, ending, tension |
| 14 | Targeted grammar and editing | Diagnose a genuine recurring issue from learner's draft; practise 5 original sentences | Sentence clarity, punctuation, deliberate register |
| 15 | Read, compare and write | Compare two original short texts, then write 180–250 words | Cross-text synthesis and independent evidence |

## Week 4 — Creative voice, discussion and consolidation

| Day | Focus | Learner output | Evidence to observe |
|---|---|---|---|
| 16 | Character and viewpoint | Create a scene in two voices (100–150 words each) | Consistency, deliberate narrative choices |
| 17 | Poetry / compressed language | Read an original short poem created for this lesson; annotate images and sound | Figurative language, multiple plausible meanings |
| 18 | Oral argument | Prepare a 2-minute spoken argument and self-review; if possible discuss with an adult | Speech organisation; **human interaction requires human evidence** |
| 19 | Writing portfolio | Choose one early draft and make a substantial revision; compare | Visible improvement and independence |
| 20 | Four-week review | New original reading task, 250-word independent writing, reflect on strengths and gaps | Repeated evidence, retention, next month plan |

## Text creation and rights policy

When a lesson needs a text that is not included in this repository, **create an original passage** of roughly 350–600 words, suitable for a capable Stage 9 first-language reader. Make it intellectually engaging, with an arguable ambiguity, credible characters and meaningful language choices. Avoid imitating or reproducing identifiable copyrighted works, exam questions, textbook passages or school-only content. Clearly mark it **Original practice passage generated for this session — not Cambridge official**.

Keep source text accessible during evidence-based questions. For every question, wait for the learner's attempt before giving targeted hints.

## Progress logic

- **After every session:** make a short private session record. Record *task status*, *evidence quality*, *help level* and *retention* separately.
- **Weekly:** revisit one earlier skill independently; a completed worksheet is not evidence of lasting mastery.
- **At four weeks:** present a parent-shareable Russian summary only if the student requests it. Do not claim access to earlier chats unless records are supplied.
- **If a day is missed:** reschedule one priority task; do not punish with streak loss or a backlog avalanche.

## Limitations

This plan cannot by itself validate all 67 historical Stage 9 codes. Speaking and listening objectives may require audio or human discussion. Final annual curriculum mapping, Oak content integration and official school requirements remain outside this pilot until source checks are complete.
