# Original English diagnostic 01 — The Last Signal

**Status:** original pilot exercise, **not** an official Cambridge test.  
**Suggested time:** 45–60 minutes. **Purpose:** collect initial evidence about reading inference, evidence, evaluation, style and independent writing.  
**Tutor:** Show the passage, then ask **one question at a time**. Keep the student's first responses before providing hints. Do not announce a secret official score.

## Reading passage (original)

### The Last Signal

At six each evening, the light on the old watchtower flashed three times across the harbour. Most people in Bellmere had forgotten why. The fishing boats had satellite radios, the harbour office had screens, and the tower itself was supposed to be empty.

But it was never dark.

On the first Monday of October, the light flashed four times.

Nora, who delivered bread before school, noticed the extra flash because she counted everything: the cracks between paving stones, the bells of passing bicycles, the seconds between thunder and rain. Her father said counting was a way of making the world seem more obedient than it was.

At the harbour office, Mr Vale laughed when she told him.

“The wiring is older than you are,” he said. “It has probably forgotten how to count.”

He wrote *faulty signal* on a form without looking up. Behind him, a map of the coast was pinned to the wall. Someone had drawn a red circle around the narrow channel east of the breakwater.

That night, the light flashed four times again. The following morning, a small boat returned with a cracked mast. Its captain said a buoy had drifted into the channel, but the harbour office announced that the accident was caused by poor visibility.

Nora went to the library. In a book of local history, she found a photograph of the watchtower taken a hundred years earlier. Beneath it was a line of tiny print: *Three flashes: all clear. Four flashes: channel unsafe. Five flashes: immediate danger.*

She copied the words onto the back of a bread receipt.

At six the next evening, Nora waited beside the harbour wall with the receipt in her hand. The sky was clear, and Mr Vale had promised to have the tower repaired. Yet the light flashed four times.

A fisherman beside her muttered, “Old machines do what they like.”

Nora thought of the red circle on the map. She thought of the captain who had come home with a broken mast. And she thought of the way Mr Vale had smiled without looking at her.

She could walk away. She was only a delivery girl, and the harbour office had people whose job was to understand these things. Instead, she walked towards the office and asked to see the map.

Mr Vale stood in the doorway. For a moment, his expression was unreadable.

Then he stepped aside.

The red circle was gone.

## Reading questions (one at a time)

1. **Literal understanding:** What changed about the watchtower signal, and why did Nora notice it? Answer in two or three sentences.
2. **Inference:** Give **two details** suggesting that the extra flash may be meaningful rather than a random fault. Explain each.
3. **Perspective:** How do Mr Vale and Nora respond differently to uncertainty? Support your comparison with details from the passage.
4. **Writer's craft:** What effect does the final sentence, *“The red circle was gone,”* create? Explain how its position at the end contributes.
5. **Evaluation:** Is Nora justified in questioning the harbour office? Give a balanced answer, including one possible counterargument.

## Independent writing (first draft)

**Prompt:** Write **200–300 words** from the perspective of the fisherman who watched Nora approach the office. Begin just after the watchtower flashes four times. Show his changing attitude towards Nora and the warning without simply retelling the original passage.

Before writing, give a **three-point outline**. Then write the first draft independently. The tutor should **not** write the paragraph or full response for you.
